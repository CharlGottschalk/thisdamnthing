Initialization patterns

Every screen in the sequence asks one thing, in one column, with the same header shape and the same footer. This page defines the frame, the ten question patterns that go inside it, and the rules that keep nine screens feeling like one.

Open the mock interview Nine screens, five groups, clickable end to end.
01 — Frame

One rail, one column, one action

288px rail on a darker surface. The question column is capped at 640px and centred in the remaining space. The footer rule is always there, even on the welcome screen, so the primary button never moves.

rail — 288
question column — max 640
Header shape

Mono eyebrow naming the group and position, 32px question at 600, then one lede under 54 characters wide. Never two paragraphs.

Vertical rhythm

26 between header and control, 30 when a screen carries two controls, 44 above the footer rule. Nothing else.

One gold thing

The primary button is gold. A selected card gets a gold hairline, not a gold fill. The rail's current step is the only other gold fill on screen.

03 — Rules

Writing the questions

Do
Ask the question as a question, ending in a question mark.
Say what the answer changes, and say it in the lede rather than a tooltip.
Name every default. "Recommended" is not a reason; give the reason.
Let a screen be skippable when it genuinely is, and label the button "Skip and continue".
Keep the whole sequence under nine screens. If it grows, group harder.
Don't
No progress percentages on the interview itself — the rail is the progress.
No two primary buttons, and no primary button that moves between screens.
Don't write anything to disk before the confirm screen, and don't imply that you have.
No jargon in a question a non-technical operator will see; keep it for the mono values.
No celebration copy. "Atlas is ready" is the whole reward.
